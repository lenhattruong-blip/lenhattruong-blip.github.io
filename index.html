A modular, low-power STM32L4 platform

An STM32L4P5 extension with a separate 2-layer power board and 4-layer MCU board, designed from schematic through PCB layout in Altium.

  • Designed both extension boards and carried out physical bring-up; the first fabricated revision operated successfully.
  • Provided SPI, I2C, UART and SWD interfaces, a 1.8 V power domain and controlled sensor power.
  • Documented the hardware with board photographs, layer drawings and STM32L4P5 voltage/current plots.
Altium DesignerSTM32L4P5PCB designLow power
Design & measurement notes+

The extension's hardware measurements are separate from my statistical analysis of the team's STM32L432 reference-board dataset. That analysis used permutation tests and Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment; it is not a Buck/LDO benchmark of this extension. The reference dataset remains private.

STM32L4P5 measurement example, 3.6 V condition. A waveform observation, not a claim of regulator efficiency or average energy savings.

ESP32-S3 greenhouse sensor integration

Migrated the sensing subsystem of a Raspberry Pi 5-based greenhouse design to ESP32-S3 N16R8, aiming to reduce cost, power consumption and system complexity.

  • Integrated environmental, soil and CO₂ sensing over I2C, UART and RS485 Modbus RTU using existing libraries.
  • Used three sensor tasks and one MQTT task with a shared-data mutex; published JSON telemetry to the application.
  • My focus was sensor acquisition and integration. Teammates handled the application and application-side automation.
ESP32-S3FreeRTOSMQTT / JSONModbus RTU
Implementation & current limitations+

The system operated on a physical setup. The published snapshot includes MQTT retries, but has not been re-tested during portfolio preparation. The source diagram uses CO₂ GPIO 15/16 while the code uses 4/5; TLS certificate verification and Wi-Fi recovery also need further work. These limitations are documented in the repository. No measured cost or power savings are claimed.

RV32IM CPU — FPGA Implementation & Verification

Implementation and waveform analysis of a team-developed CPU, comparing nine adder/multiplier configurations in Vivado. My contribution focuses on implementation reports, timing/resource analysis and simulation debugging.

  • Reviewed routed timing and resource reports targeting the Zynq-7000 xc7z030ffg676-1.
  • Inspected BRAM control-state sequencing, program-counter progression and ALU behavior through XSim waveforms.
  • Published selected reports and diagrams as an evidence-only portfolio; team RTL and testbench source remain private.
VivadoXSimTiming analysisRV32IM
Architecture & evidence boundaries+

The BRAM core uses a sequencer and iterative division, so it is not uniformly single-cycle. Historical timing and utilization results come from separate runs and were not regenerated for this portfolio. Physical FPGA-board operation, full ISA compliance and power-efficiency rankings are not claimed. The diagrams describe the team's design, not sole authorship.

COMPUTER VISION · TEAM COURSE PROJECTMAY — AUG 2026

Vietnamese traffic-sign detection with YOLO26s

Took primary responsibility for finding, analyzing and combining two datasets. Co-developed Python recognition and tested inference with teammate Thanh, using weights trained by another team member.

  • The report records 10,172 images, 17,860 annotated objects and 66 configured classes.
  • Team-reported test mAP@0.5: 0.987 on 1,018 test images. Two classes have no evaluation samples.
  • Evidence-only portfolio: dataset summary and evaluation results, without source, weights or the full report.
PythonOpenCVYOLO26sDataset preparation
Evaluation & deployment limits+

Results are reported by the team, not independently rerun. Evaluation represents 64 classes; related video frames across splits remain a possible source of optimistic scores. Test mAP@0.5:0.95 was not retained. Webcam FPS observations are not a standardized benchmark, and Raspberry Pi 5 deployment remains planned. Street photographs are withheld pending image-provenance review.
